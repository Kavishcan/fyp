| 6 | Security, source trust, and adversarial robustness | 7, 21-22, 24, 35, 50 | Routing Hijacking (21) \| https://www.semanticscholar.org/search?q=Targeted%20Routing%20Hijacking%20Federated%20RAG <br>PoisonedRAG (50) \| https://www.semanticscholar.org/search?q=PoisonedRAG <br>Corrective RAG (35) \| https://www.semanticscholar.org/search?q=Corrective%20Retrieval%20Augmented%20Generation <br>Securing RAG Taxonomy (24) \| https://www.semanticscholar.org/search?q=Securing%20Retrieval-Augmented%20Generation%20Taxonomy | How RAG behaves when documents, source metadata, relevance signals, or participating clients are malicious, unreliable, or manipulated. | Research examines knowledge poisoning, routing hijacking, prompt injection, unreliable evidence, corrective retrieval, source-reliability mechanisms, and evidence verification. | Distributed RAG introduces another attack surface because independently controlled sources may manipulate their content or advertised relevance.
