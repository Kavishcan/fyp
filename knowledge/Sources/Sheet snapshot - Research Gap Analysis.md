| 1 | Privacy-aware source routing | Federated source routing + RAG privacy | RAGRoute selects a smaller set of relevant sources to reduce communication. Privacy papers describe leakage from queries and retrieved context. Existing work does not clearly demonstrate privacy-aware routing at very large client counts. | RAGRoute: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19280 <br>The Good and The Bad: https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.267/ <br>FedE4RAG: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19101 | Routing is treated mainly as an efficiency problem. A flat router can become costly as the client pool grows, while the query, relevant client identity and retrieval patterns may still leak. Evidence at 1,000+ clients is also limited. | Build a two-stage privacy-aware router. Candidate selection first reduces 30-1,000 virtual clients to about 20-50 candidates; a privacy- and trust-aware reranker then contacts only the best 3-10 sources. Start with 30+ real or local clients and evaluate larger pools through controlled simulation. | Supports cross-silo healthcare or enterprise QA where institutions cannot expose all documents or reveal who holds relevant information. | A scalable two-stage routing method that balances source relevance, privacy leakage, source trust, communication cost and answer quality. | Maintaining routing recall, privacy and low latency as the number of clients increases, while avoiding an unsupported claim of 1,000 physical deployments. | Combines privacy-aware source selection with hierarchical scaling and source trust. The router is evaluated with 30+ practical clients and progressively larger virtual pools up to 1,000 clients. | High | Routing, privacy protection and scalable candidate selection exist separately, but their joint optimisation with source trust is weakly covered. The 1,000-client capability remains an evaluation target until experimentally validated. | Evaluate client pools of 30, 100, 300 and up to 1,000 virtual clients using source-selection Recall@K, routing latency, clients contacted, communication volume, answer quality, source-inference attack success and malicious-client selection rate. |
| 2 | Robust routing against malicious or misleading sources | Federated routing security + knowledge poisoning | Routing-hijacking and poisoned-RAG studies show that malicious sources can attract queries or insert misleading evidence. | Routing hijacking: https://www.semanticscholar.org/search?q=Targeted%20Routing%20Hijacking%20in%20Federated%20RAG <br>PoisonedRAG: https://www.semanticscholar.org/search?q=PoisonedRAG%20Knowledge%20Corruption%20Attacks | Efficient or private routing can still choose a malicious client. Privacy-preserving source profiles also make direct inspection harder. | Add trust history, anomaly signals and evidence consistency to the privacy-aware routing score, while limiting how much client information is exposed. | Important for medical, legal and enterprise systems where one compromised source can produce a high-impact wrong answer. | A trust-aware privacy-preserving router with attack simulation and source quarantine or down-ranking. | Detecting adaptive attackers without unfairly rejecting small or unusual honest clients. | Joins routing privacy with source trust and attack resilience in one selection mechanism. | High | Security papers expose the attack and routing papers optimise selection, but the combined privacy-and-trust router remains underexplored. | Attack success rate, malicious-client selection rate, clean-source Recall@K, answer degradation and false-positive rate. |
| 3 | End-to-end privacy across the FedRAG inference pipeline | Privacy-preserving RAG + confidential FedRAG | FedE4RAG protects federated retriever learning. C-FedRAG uses confidential computing. HyFedRAG handles heterogeneous sensitive data. | FedE4RAG: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19101 <br>C-FedRAG: https://www.semanticscholar.org/search?q=C-FedRAG%20Confidential%20Federated%20Retrieval-Augmented%20Generation <br>HyFedRAG: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06444 | Most systems protect one or two stages. Query text, routing metadata, embeddings, retrieved passages, prompts and generated outputs are not evaluated together. | Define one threat model for the complete query-to-answer path, then apply local redaction, access control, minimal context sharing and output checks at the correct stages. | Gives sensitive organisations a clearer way to decide what remains local and what may be shared during question answering. | An auditable inference pipeline with privacy controls and leakage measurements at routing, retrieval, prompting and output. | Every extra protection can reduce evidence quality, increase latency or depend on trusted infrastructure. | Evaluates privacy as a pipeline property rather than as a single protected component. | High | The reviewed frameworks are close to the topic, but no one framework clearly measures and protects every inference-stage asset under one evaluation. | Query/document leakage rate, embedding or source inference, output leakage, groundedness, answer quality and end-to-end latency. |
| 4 | Unified evaluation of quality, privacy, robustness and efficiency | FedRAG evaluation | Ragas, ARES and RAGChecker evaluate retrieval and answer quality. Federated-search work reports routing accuracy and communication savings. | Ragas: https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15217 <br>ARES: https://www.semanticscholar.org/search?q=ARES%20Automated%20Evaluation%20Framework%20for%20RAG <br>RAGChecker: https://www.semanticscholar.org/search?q=RAGChecker%20Fine-grained%20Framework | Studies usually report only their main objective. Privacy leakage, source-selection quality, answer faithfulness, malicious-client robustness and communication cost are rarely reported together. | Create one repeatable evaluation protocol covering routing, retrieval, generation, privacy attacks, robustness, latency and bandwidth. | Makes comparisons more meaningful for real distributed deployments instead of reporting answer accuracy alone. | A multi-objective FedRAG scorecard with common baselines and ablation tests for each protection. | Metrics may conflict, and LLM-based evaluators can be variable or expose sensitive context. | The novelty is mainly experimental: a single protocol for evaluating the full privacy-aware routing trade-off. | Medium-High | Most individual metrics already exist, but their combined use for privacy-aware FedRAG routing is a useful contribution. | Recall@K, MRR/nDCG, EM/F1, faithfulness, leakage/attack success, malicious-source rate, latency and bytes transferred. |
| 5 | Realistic heterogeneous privacy-sensitive FedRAG benchmark | Federated datasets + benchmark design | FeB4RAG supplies distributed retrieval pools, MIRAGE supplies medical QA, and MultiHop-RAG supplies multi-source reasoning. | FeB4RAG/RAGRoute: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19280 <br>MIRAGE: https://github.com/gzxiong/MIRAGE <br>MultiHop-RAG: https://huggingface.co/datasets/yixuantt/MultiHopRAG | Public benchmarks do not naturally include private client identities, access rules, realistic PII, malicious sources and controlled overlap between institutions. | Create a reproducible 6-8 client healthcare case study using public medical corpora, controlled non-sensitive synthetic identifiers, client access rules and injected attack cases. | Provides a realistic evaluation setting without using real patient records or claiming that public QA data contains PII. | A client-partition generator, privacy annotations, attack fixtures and repeatable splits that work with the routing framework. | Synthetic privacy cases may not represent every real institution, while large medical corpora have storage and licensing constraints. | The contribution is the controlled federated evaluation setup and its privacy/security annotations, not a new medical dataset collected from patients. | Medium-High | The source datasets already exist, but the reviewed work lacks this exact controlled combination for privacy-aware routing experiments. | Client imbalance and overlap reports, routing labels, privacy leakage, attack success, retrieval/answer quality and missing-client robustness. |
